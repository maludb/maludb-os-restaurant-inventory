# Restaurant inventory software — a field survey

Scope: what the shipping products do, screen by screen, for purchase orders, receiving, recipes, sales depletion, waste, counts, projection and costing. Every claim carries a URL; a source is marked **(vendor marketing)** when it is the product's own promotional page rather than help documentation, a review, or a third party. Several help centres (Fourth/HotSchedules, Yellow Dog, MarginEdge, Toast's product page, Opsi's Toast listing) refused automated fetches with 403; what is reported from them comes from search-engine extracts of those pages, cited by URL.

## 0. The findings, in short

1. **The unit model is three-tiered everywhere, anchored on three measure types.** Restaurant365 is explicit: every unit of measure must equate, directly or by a chain, to one of *volume* (OZ-fl), *weight* (OZ-wt) or *each*; a purchased item carries a fixed measure type, an inventory U of M, purchase U of Ms, recipe U of Ms, a reporting U of M and up to two extra count U of Ms (https://docs.restaurant365.com/docs/unit-of-measure, https://docs.restaurant365.com/docs/inventory-counts-using-multiple-count-u-of-ms). Craftable prices everything at a "CU price" (count unit) and recipes consume an "RU size" (recipe unit) (https://help.craftable.com/learning/understanding-yields). The one trap every product documents: a case defined as "1 each" instead of "100/each" (R365's oyster example).
2. **Theoretical vs actual is the same formula in every product**, differing only in what they subtract. Compeat/R365: Theo End = Beg + Receiving + Xfers In + Prod/Portioning − Xfers Out − Theo Usage; Variance = Actual End − Theo End; uncounted items have variance zero by definition (https://cloud.compeat.com/CompeatBackOffice/Help/reports_inv/Inventory_Count_Variance.htm). Craftable: Theoretical = Start + Received + Transferred − Manual Depletions − POS Depletions, with waste a manual depletion that can be toggled in or out of the report (https://help.craftable.com/learning/actual-vs-theoretical-quantity-details-report).
3. **R365 chose periodic, not perpetual, inventory on purpose** — purchases are expensed to COGS at once and a posted count *is* the inventory journal; the stated reason is that a purchased item has no one-to-one link to a sold item (https://docs.restaurant365.com/docs/periodic-inventory). MarketMan and Apicbase sell "perpetual" (POS depletes a running balance) (https://www.marketman.com/perpetual-inventory, vendor marketing). A design must pick one and say which number the screens show.
4. **Suggested ordering is par-minus-projected-stock, with on-order included.** Craftable's published rule: an item is below par when Par > In Stock + New + Placed; Goal Qty = Par − In Stock − Placed − New, rounded to the purchase unit needing the fewest units, exceeding the goal by at most 20 %, never placed automatically; no pars by day of week or season (https://help.craftable.com/learning/setting-up-par-levels). Supy and MarketMan add a 15-day item-level sales forecast run through recipes (https://supy.io/product-features/ai-predictive-ordering, vendor marketing).
5. **Forecasting, where it exists, is sales history × recipes ÷ yield + safety stock.** MarginEdge's own method: 12 months of daily item sales by daypart, weekly rhythm and seasonality, convert to ingredients through recipes, divide by yield (8 oz cooked ÷ 0.78 = 10.3 oz raw), category safety stock (proteins 20–30 %, dairy 15–20 %, dry 8–15 %), compare to on-hand, last order and the rolling 4-week average (https://www.marginedge.com/blog/how-to-forecast-restaurant-inventory-from-sales-data-in-7-steps). Weather and events appear as manager adjustments (Supy) or marketing claims (Yokitup), not as documented models.
6. **Yield lives in two places and the products keep them apart.** Craftable: *item yield* (trim on receipt, changes the CU price — 100 lb at $100 at 90 % becomes 90 lb at $1.11) vs *recipe yield %* per ingredient (changes depletion, not cost) plus a *recipe size override* used for sub-recipe depletion (https://help.craftable.com/learning/understanding-yields). Edify's formula is the industry's: raw = quantity ÷ (1 − loss %) (https://intercom.help/edify-systems/en/articles/15741188-creating-and-editing-recipes).
7. **Catch weight is "order by each, receive and count by weight".** Fourth/HotSchedules: a 5 kg joint ordered as one each at £4/kg, received at 5.3 kg, the delivered cost recalculated; the invoice UoM and the count UoM must be the same weight unit; counts in purchase units convert by the item's *average weight* (https://help.hotschedules.com/hc/en-us/articles/360042714151-Inventory-Restaurants-Ordering-Receiving-and-Counting-Catch-Weight-Products-in-Inventory, https://help.hotschedules.com/hc/en-us/community/posts/7486814147725-July-12th-14th-2022-Release-Note-Adaco-Ability-to-Count-Inventory-in-Sub-Pack-Units-Calculation-of-Counted-Catch-Weight-Items).
8. **Counts are shelf-to-sheet lists per storage area, with partial units three ways:** a case plus loose sub-units converted to a fraction (WISK: 1 case of 15 cartons + 1 carton + 5 eggs = 16.42 cartons, https://help.wisk.ai/en/articles/3533148-unit-based-items), a tenths slider for open bottles (Backbar, https://www.getbackbar.com/support/how-to-use-bottle-slider-inventory), or weight against empty/full bottle weights (BevSpot, https://bevspot.com/blog/accurately-calculate-keg-bottle-weights-bevspot/). Toast asks what to do with blank rows: cancel, count as 0, or skip (https://support.toasttab.com/en/article/Get-Started-with-Toast-Inventory).
9. **Invoice capture is the moat of the invoice-first products** — MarginEdge promises line items within 24–48 h including handwritten notes, via photo, email, upload or EDI, links the same SKU across vendors, and emails on a per-item price threshold (https://www.marginedge.com/how-it-works, vendor marketing). xtraCHEF is the same shape inside Toast, with "products" required before counts or recipes (https://intercom.help/toast-central/en/articles/14727601-xtrachef-101).
10. **Commissary = transfer that becomes an invoice.** R365: the commissary depletes ingredients, the destination receives one "Comm-" finished item, accepting a transfer posts an AP invoice numbered like the transfer, with an optional surcharge % (https://radar.help.restaurant365.com/support/solutions/articles/12000097845-inventory-commissary-transfers-as-invoices).
11. **Distributor portals now ship their own inventory worksheets** (US Foods MOXē: worksheets from the order guide, groups = storage areas, last-price or market-price valuation, a food-cost calculator; Sysco's partner tool: storage locations, multi-unit conversion, order-guide price updates at $135/month) — the order guide is the item master a small operator already has (https://www.usfoods.com/how-we-help-you/easy-ordering/inventory, https://www.solutions.sysco.com/hospitality-innovations, vendor marketing).
12. **What users complain about is setup and discipline, not features**: labour-intensive catalog/recipe/unit setup, count sync lag in walk-ins, mobile apps weaker than desktop, support slow on hard issues, price ($239–$499/location/month), and reports whose arithmetic is not explained (https://www.softwareadvice.com/scm/marketman-restaurant-management-profile/, https://www.capterra.com/p/200434/Craftable/reviews/, https://www.selecthub.com/restaurant-management-software/marginedge-vs-restaurant365/). No r/restaurateur or r/KitchenConfidential thread surfaced in four searches; the complaints below are from Capterra, G2, Software Advice, Trustpilot and SelectHub.

## 1. The products, one by one

**MarketMan** (https://www.marketman.com/, vendor marketing). Inventory, suppliers, ordering, recipes and perpetual stock: enter items, recipes, menus and pars; POS sales subtract ingredients and supplier orders add purchases to a running balance; counts by barcode, by hand or on shelf-to-sheet sheets that "mimic your storage"; alerts when stock falls under par and optional automatic reorder (https://www.marketman.com/perpetual-inventory). Suggestive Ordering claims to use sales history, recipes, reported waste and trends (https://www.marketman.com/blog/review-an-analytics-on-the-go-with-saasy-mobile). Its API lists recipes, inventory items and purchase orders as separate entities but does not publish the unit model (https://supergood.ai/docs/marketman-api). Square resells it as "Square Restaurant Inventory by MarketMan" since April 2026 (https://www.nasdaq.com/press-release/square-and-marketman-launch-advanced-inventory-management-integration-restaurants). Entry pricing reported at $239/location/month (G2) or $199/user/month (Capterra) — the sources disagree (https://g2.com/products/marketman/pricing, https://www.capterra.com/p/136439/Marketman-Restaurant-Inventory).

**Restaurant365, with its Compeat lineage.** R365 is accounting plus operations for multi-unit groups (74 % of its G2 reviewers are enterprises, https://g2.com/compare/marketman-vs-restaurant365). The documented model: periodic inventory (https://docs.restaurant365.com/docs/periodic-inventory); three measure types with chained equivalences and Purchase/Recipe flags on each U of M (https://docs.restaurant365.com/docs/unit-of-measure); a Measurement Conversions toggle for items whose units cross measure types (https://docs.restaurant365.com/docs/unit-of-measure-conversions); count templates with Storage Location folders, items dragged into shelf order (https://docs.restaurant365.com/docs/shelf-to-sheet-inventory); up to three count U of Ms per item summed into the reporting unit (romaine: 2 cases + 5 three-count bags + 1 chopped bag) (https://docs.restaurant365.com/docs/inventory-counts-using-multiple-count-u-of-ms); an approved count becomes the inventory journal, the first count sets the balance sheet (https://docs.restaurant365.com/docs/first-inventory-count, https://docs.restaurant365.com/docs/counts); Days on Hand = ((Start $ + End $)/2) / (Period Cost of Sales / Days) (https://radar.help.restaurant365.com/support/solutions/articles/12000083927-days-on-hand); the commissary module with order templates per delivery day and mass fulfilment (https://docs.restaurant365.com/docs/classic-commissary) and transfers posting as AP invoices (https://radar.help.restaurant365.com/support/solutions/articles/12000097845-inventory-commissary-transfers-as-invoices). Compeat's reports, still online, are the clearest published arithmetic: Cost of Goods (theoretical $ = weighted average cost × theoretical units; weighted average = Σ$ ÷ Σunits over the range; only "Controllable Cost" items appear; optional "theoretical waste/donation usage") (https://cloud.compeat.com/CompeatBackOffice/Help/Cost_Of_Goods.htm), Actual Usage (prior count + purchases − ending count; theoretical count substitutes for a missing count) (https://cloud.compeat.com/CompeatBackOffice/Help/reports_inv/Inventory_Actual_Usage.htm) and Count Variance (columns Beg Inv, Receiving, Xfers, Prod/Portioning, Waste, Theo Usage, Theo End Inv, Actual End Inv, Actual Usage, Variance, Var %; mismatched date ranges accepted but "less reliable") (https://cloud.compeat.com/CompeatBackOffice/Help/reports_inv/Inventory_Count_Variance.htm). Reported pricing from $499/location/month (https://www.selecthub.com/restaurant-management-software/marginedge-vs-restaurant365/).

**MarginEdge** (https://www.marginedge.com/how-it-works, vendor marketing). Invoice-first: photo/email/upload/EDI in, line items out in 24–48 h; SKUs related across vendors; coding rules; per-item price thresholds that email; count sheets that gain new products automatically with centrally controlled count-by units and storage-area sheets (walk-in, dry, bar) (https://help.marginedge.com/hc/en-us/articles/360057072773-MarginEdge-At-A-Glance-An-Overview); order guides sorted by category, alphabet or sheet-to-shelf with pars and on-hand; recipes on tablets with yield conversions and prep labels; the Theoretical Usage report needs recipes, PMIX and two closed inventories and warns when POS lines are unmapped; menu analysis as Stars/Puzzles/Plow Horses/Dogs; bill pay included. Reported $300–$350/location/month (https://g2.com/compare/marginedge-vs-restaurant365).

**xtraCHEF by Toast** (https://intercom.help/toast-central/en/articles/14727601-xtrachef-101). Two tiers: Essentials (invoice automation, accounting sync, class/location tracking) and Pro (recipe costing, inventory, analytics). Invoice line items become "products"; products are required before any count or recipe; recipes link to the Toast menu for plate cost and prime cost; counts feed depletion/waste/shrinkage analytics; US only. A third party notes recipe management is thin for batch, multi-level and commissary work (https://us.fitgap.com/products/xtrachef). Toast's own (non-xtraCHEF) Inventory, in beta outside the US, is the plainest reference model: an Ingredient Library imported from a sheet with primary and secondary units, container sizes, conversion, unit price, par, supplier, pack size and storage area; storage areas *are* the count lists; counts per area with "out of stock"; the blank-row prompt; reorder suggestions = items below par or out, refreshed on delivery; each ingredient linked to a supplier with pack configuration, item code and pack price (https://support.toasttab.com/en/article/Get-Started-with-Toast-Inventory). Toast Retail's cross-location transfers require item matching at both sites and switch tracking on for transferred items (https://support.toasttab.com/en/article/Toast-Retail-Utilize-Cross-Location-Transfers).

**Craftable (Bevager/Foodager)**. The best-documented arithmetic of the group (above: pars, AvT, yields). Items have a CU (count unit) price and an RU (recipe unit) size; pars have a threshold plus optional preferred vendor/purchase unit/quantity; bulk par editing by category and vendor in Item Manager (https://help.craftable.com/learning/setting-up-par-levels). Capterra reviewers of 2020 said Foodager "is not explicit in how it calculates numbers" and wanted one recipe pool across the bar and food halves (https://www.capterra.com/p/200434/Craftable/reviews/?page=3). Pricing by quote (https://g2.com/products/craftable/pricing). TC Restaurant Group's 5 % company-wide variance is a vendor case study (https://craftable.com/blog/tc-restaurant-group).

**Apicbase** (European, F&B groups). Recipes and master data with cost, margin, allergens and nutrition per recipe/menu/location; stock adjusted per dish sold; minimum-stock alerts; a barcode scanner app with "Partial Counts" status and a "Record Waste" module; procurement from stock levels (https://softguide.com/program/apicbase, https://mwm.ai/apps/apicbase/1553503445, https://www.g2.com/products/https-get-apicbase-com/discuss). Its yield/AP-EP help pages did not surface in search.

**Yellow Dog Inventory**. **BlueCart** (marketplace + inventory). Pars that alert or auto-generate a PO; predictive ordering from historical depletion, current levels and seasonality (https://www.bluecart.com/blog/predictive-ordering-for-restaurants, vendor marketing; https://www.cbinsights.com/investor/improvonia). **Supy** (Gulf/UK, Foodics POS): 15-day item-level sales forecast by branch, manager adjustments for events, recipes convert it to ingredient need over a coverage period, on-hand subtracted and editable at order time, projected stock on delivery date shown, one draft PO per supplier, last order and 4-week average alongside, sent by integration/email/WhatsApp, nothing automatic (https://supy.io/product-features/ai-predictive-ordering, vendor marketing). **Galley Solutions** ("Culinary Resource Planning"; Aramark, DoorDash, Hyatt claimed). ## 2. Items and units

- **Three measure types, chained equivalences** (R365): "Case – 4 PKG (5 LB)" = 320 OZ-wt directly, or = 20 Pound = 320 OZ-wt indirectly; a U of M has Name, Equivalent U of M, Equivalent Qty, flags Purchase (default on) and Recipe (default off) (https://docs.restaurant365.com/docs/unit-of-measure). Cross-type conversions (a volume unit for an item kept by weight) are per item, behind a toggle, and apply to recipes too (https://docs.restaurant365.com/docs/unit-of-measure-conversions).
- **Four roles for a unit** (Easilys names them billing, order, storage, production; R365 purchase, inventory, recipe, reporting) (https://support.dev.mapal-os.com/hc/en-gb/articles/34494680711442-Understand-product-billing-ordering-storage-and-production-units, https://docs.restaurant365.com/docs/unit-of-measure).
- **Vendor items are distinct from products**: xtraCHEF's invoice lines become products; MarginEdge relates SKUs across vendors; R365's commissary creates "vendor purchase items" that must be linked to inventory items (unassigned-VPI alert) (https://intercom.help/toast-central/en/articles/14727601-xtrachef-101, https://www.marginedge.com/how-it-works, https://radar.help.restaurant365.com/support/solutions/articles/12000097845-inventory-commissary-transfers-as-invoices). Toast's supplier link holds item name, pack configuration, item code and pack price (https://support.toasttab.com/en/article/Get-Started-with-Toast-Inventory).
- **Catch weight**: finding 7. Supy's blog on unit conversion attributes much variance to missing conversions (https://supy.io/blog/learn-restaurant-inventory-unit-conversion, vendor).

## 3. Vendors, order guides, pars and suggested orders

Order guides are the distributors' native object (Sysco: history, bid guides, par lists, custom guides; US Foods: order guide, master list, custom lists). MarginEdge's guides update themselves from invoices and carry pars and on-hand (https://www.marginedge.com/how-it-works). Craftable's rule (finding 4) is the only fully published one; Toast's list is simply "below par or out of stock" (https://support.toasttab.com/en/article/Get-Started-with-Toast-Inventory). The common third-party par formula: par = average daily usage × lead-time days + safety stock (https://www.nory.ai/blog/how-to-calculate-par-level-in-a-restaurant); R365's blog frames it as covering the gap between deliveries plus a cushion (https://www.restaurant365.com/blog/how-to-set-up-par-levels-and-reorder-alerts-with-suppliers/). Clearview computes "stock on hand" and "build to (par)" from sales history and projected sales (https://clearviewconnect.com/suggested-ordering). Supy shows projected stock on the delivery date per line and lets the user correct on-hand inside the order (https://supy.io/product-features/ai-predictive-ordering).

## 4. Invoice capture and receiving

MarginEdge (24–48 h human-assisted extraction incl. handwriting, EDI, price alerts, bill pay) and xtraCHEF (AI/ML extraction, approvals, accounting sync, Ingredient Price Tracker) define the category; both treat the invoice as the source of item cost (https://www.marginedge.com/how-it-works, https://intercom.help/toast-central/en/articles/14727601-xtrachef-101). Fourth's receiving edits the delivered quantity with a reason code ("catch weight") and recalculates delivered cost (https://help.hotschedules.com/hc/en-us/articles/360042712731-Release-Notes-P2P-I-Catch-Weight-Items). Compeat counts "purchases" as AP invoices, manual checks, portion cut sheets or prep-item postings (https://cloud.compeat.com/CompeatBackOffice/Help/reports_inv/Inventory_Count_Variance.htm). Contract-vs-invoice drift is a recognised problem: invoices "very commonly" differ from contracted prices (https://www.nrn.com/node/36358); MarginEdge's Price Movers and R365's discrepancy detection answer it (https://www.marginedge.com/food-cost, https://www.restaurant365.com/blog/how-can-restaurant-operators-respond-quickly-to-price-fluctuations/). No product documents distributor pricing tiers (bid/contract/market) as a data object; MOXē's worksheet offers *last purchase price* vs *market price* as valuation choices (https://www.usfoods.com/how-we-help-you/easy-ordering/inventory).

## 5. Recipes, sub-recipes, yield, plate cost, menu engineering

Recursive BOMs are universal (OpenKitchen, Galley, Craftable, Meez, ti-ext-restapro). Craftable's yield split (finding 6) is the most precise statement of AP/EP: trim on receipt changes the count-unit cost; yield in a recipe changes the quantity consumed; a sub-recipe's total size drives how much of each ingredient depletes. Foodics defines yield % = net ÷ gross × 100, waste = gross − net (https://help.foodics.com/hc/en-us/articles/6736380238748); Nutritics: a 90 % onion yield means 1000 g bought, 900 g enters the recipe, and changing the yield re-costs every recipe using it (https://www.nutritics.com/p/userguide&c=601_foods and recipes); a Portuguese study measured ~18 % average trim on meat and found yield testing routinely skipped (https://revistas.rcaap.pt/thij/article/download/47934/33029/237928). Costing uses weighted average cost (Compeat) or latest invoice cost (xtraCHEF, OpenKitchen); R365 falls back to the item's Inv. Unit Cost if never purchased (https://cloud.compeat.com/CompeatBackOffice/Help/Cost_Of_Goods.htm). Menu engineering: contribution margin = price − plate cost; popularity = share of category sales, "popular" at ≥ 70 % of an equal split; Stars/Plowhorses/Puzzles/Dogs (https://www.getmeez.com/blog/menu-engineering-matrix, https://pos.toasttab.com/zh-us/blog/on-the-line/menu-engineering-matrix). MarginEdge and Craftable ship it as a report.

## 6. POS integration and theoretical depletion

Every product maps POS buttons *and modifiers* to recipes; unmapped lines are the known failure (MarginEdge warns; Craftable says re-run depletions if mapping changes mid-period) (https://help.marginedge.com/hc/en-us/articles/360057072773-MarginEdge-At-A-Glance-An-Overview, https://help.craftable.com/learning/actual-vs-theoretical-quantity-details-report). Compeat expands prep items into ingredients only when asked ("Expand Non-produced, Non-Commissary Prep Items") (https://cloud.compeat.com/CompeatBackOffice/Help/Cost_Of_Goods.htm). MarginEdge's forecasting advice: exclude voids, keep comps — the kitchen used the food (https://www.marginedge.com/blog/how-to-forecast-restaurant-inventory-from-sales-data-in-7-steps).

## 7. Counts

Storage areas as ordered count lists (R365, MarginEdge, Toast, Yellow Dog, Sysco's tool, MOXē groups); multiple count units per item summed to one (R365 three; Sysco "pounds and cases"); sub-units as fractions (WISK); tenths or weight for bottles; blank rows asked about (Toast); printed sheets keyed later (Yellow Dog); counts offline with later sync (xtraCHEF claims, MOXē); sessions per location totalled (Sysco's tool); a count reviewed with alert flags before approval and approval posting the journal (R365); uncounted items valued at theoretical with an asterisk, variance zero (Compeat). Cycle (partial) counting: Toast Retail keeps one sheet per item with per-location lines rolled up (https://support.toasttab.com/en/article/Use-Cycle-Counts-to-Manage-Retail-Inventory). R365 lists what makes counts lie: trim, inaccurate measures, waste, POS customisations and discounts, "subjective units of measure", inconsistent deliveries (https://docs.restaurant365.com/docs/periodic-inventory).

## 8. Waste, transfers, commissary, period close

Waste is a manual depletion with a reason (Craftable, Leanpath's reason list); Compeat can count waste/donations inside theoretical usage so that logged waste does not show as variance. Transfers: net in/out per store, usage lands at the receiving store (Compeat); R365 commissary = transfer → AP invoice (+ surcharge); Toast requires item matching per location. Period close: R365's count approval is the journal, purchases are COGS when bought, month-end counts "true up" COGS (https://docs.restaurant365.com/docs/what-do-i-do-if-i-skipped-inventory-counts); MarginEdge's usage reports update when an inventory is *closed*; US Foods' calculator uses only *completed* worksheets as beginning and ending (https://www.usfoods.com/how-we-help-you/easy-ordering/inventory).

## 9. Forecasting, as it actually is

Documented inputs: daily item sales by daypart ≥ 12 months, day-of-week rhythm, seasonality vs prior year, local events (MarginEdge method); a 15-day item forecast with manual event/promotion edits (Supy); "stock on hand" and "build-to" from sales history (Clearview); weather and holidays as claimed inputs (Yokitup, https://www.yokitup.com/en/forecasts, vendor; Avero's examples are illustrative, https://averoinc.com/?p=1575). Supy's own caveat is that ML forecasting pays at 15+ locations; MAPE claims 3–7 % are self-reported (https://supy.io/blog/learn-restaurant-sales-forecasting). Opsi forecasts *prep counts*, the kitchen-facing form of the same number. Nobody publishes a model beyond "history × recipe ÷ yield + buffer − on hand − on order".

## 11. What users complain about

- **Setup is the cost**: catalog, recipes, unit conversions and vendor items are "labor-intensive"; worth it only "if you are willing to invest the initial time" (https://www.softwareadvice.com/scm/marketman-restaurant-management-profile/). MarginEdge "depends on staff committing to daily invoice submission and regular inventory counts; otherwise the data quality suffers" (https://www.selecthub.com/restaurant-management-software/marginedge-vs-marketman/).
- **Counting in a walk-in**: count sync "can occasionally lag if connectivity drops in walk-in fridges or basements"; MarketMan's mobile app "less polished than the desktop"; "loading process kinda slow" (https://www.softwareadvice.com/scm/marketman-restaurant-management-profile/, https://www.capterra.com/p/136439/Marketman-Restaurant-Inventory/reviews/?page=4). MarginEdge sells counting "even in sketchy walk-in wifi" because of it (https://www.marginedge.com/inventory-management).
- **Opaque arithmetic**: Foodager "is not explicit in how it calculates numbers; reports … redundant unwieldy layout" (https://www.capterra.com/p/200434/Craftable/reviews/?page=3). A buyer's guide warns some tools "put variance reporting behind a paywall" and that variance says something was lost but not where or why (https://www.tryotter.com/blog/restaurant-tips/restaurant-inventory-software-ranked, https://orbisk.com/blog/restaurant-food-cost-software/).
- **Two halves that do not share**: bar and food recipes in separate pools (Craftable, 2020); R365 "lacks the robust bar inventory features" per a competitor (https://www.marginedge.com/blog/marginedge-vs-restaurant365-for-independent-restaurant-operators, vendor).
- **Cost and support**: $239–$499 per location per month; support "mixed … complex issues sometimes taking days" (https://www.itqlick.com/marketman, https://www.selecthub.com/restaurant-management-software/marginedge-vs-restaurant365/); MarketMan's Spanish Trustpilot at 2.4 with unanswered negatives (https://es.trustpilot.com/review/marketman.com); R365 bank/credit-card feed connectivity needing manual fixes (SelectHub, above).
- **Unit and conversion limits**: Meez — "limitations to how you add and convert ingredients" (https://www.softwareadvice.com.au/software/344401/meez); xtraCHEF thin for batch and multi-level recipes (https://us.fitgap.com/products/xtrachef).
- **Not found**: no Reddit thread surfaced for r/restaurateur or r/KitchenConfidential in four phrasings; treat the above as the review-site record only.

## 12. Vocabulary and formulas

- **COGS / cost of goods used** = Beginning inventory + Purchases − Ending inventory (R365 https://docs.restaurant365.com/docs/periodic-inventory; US Foods adds ÷ Food sales for the %, https://www.usfoods.com/how-we-help-you/easy-ordering/inventory). Compeat: + transfers in + production − transfers out (https://cloud.compeat.com/CompeatBackOffice/Help/Cost_Of_Goods.htm).
- **Food cost %** = COGS ÷ food sales; per dish = plate cost ÷ menu price (https://beancount.io/blog/2026/07/08/menu-engineering-food-cost-percentage-stars-plowhorses-puzzles-dogs-matrix).
- **Prime cost** = COGS + labour (wages, benefits, payroll taxes); prime cost % = prime cost ÷ sales (https://www.restaurant365.com/?p=39424, https://www.fourth.com/article/restaurant-prime-cost).
- **Actual usage** (units or $) = Beg + Purchases (+ transfers in + production) − End (Compeat; "based on a physical inventory count").
- **Theoretical usage** = Σ sales qty × recipe qty per unit, prep items expanded; theoretical $ = weighted average cost × units, weighted average = Σ$ ÷ Σunits for the range (Compeat).
- **Theoretical ending inventory** = Beg + Receiving + Xfers in + Production − Xfers out − Theo usage (− waste, Craftable); **variance** = Actual end − Theo end; negative = actual lower (over-portioning, theft, missed invoices), positive = miscount, bad receiving or recipe error; **Var %** = variance ÷ theo usage or ÷ theo end (Compeat), ÷ actual or ÷ depletions (Craftable); **variance value** = variance × CU price (Craftable).
- **Par** = the in-stock threshold that triggers a reorder; third-party: average daily usage × lead-time days + safety stock (https://www.nory.ai/blog/how-to-calculate-par-level-in-a-restaurant). **Goal order qty** = Par − In stock − Placed − New (Craftable).
- **Sitting inventory**: no product defines the term; glossaries use "inventory sitting too long" for low turns (https://blackboxintelligence.com/resources/restaurant-glossary/inventory-turnover-ratio/). Use "on-hand value".
- **Inventory turns** = COGS ÷ average inventory, average = (Beg + End) ÷ 2 (https://www.bluecart.com/blog/inventory-turnover-formula). **Days on hand** = ((Start $ + End $) ÷ 2) ÷ (Period cost of sales ÷ days in period) (R365); equivalently days in period ÷ turns (https://blog.petpooja.com/glossary/inventory-turnover/).
- **Yield %** = net (EP) ÷ gross (AP) × 100; waste = gross − net (Foodics); raw needed = EP qty ÷ yield (MarginEdge 8 oz ÷ 0.78) or = qty ÷ (1 − loss %) (Edify); EP cost per unit = AP cost per unit ÷ yield (Craftable's $1.00 → $1.11 at 90 %).
- **Contribution margin** = menu price − plate cost; **popularity** = item share of category sales, popular at ≥ 70 % of an even split (Meez/Toast menu-engineering pages).
- **Catch weight** = an item purchased as a count but priced, received and counted by weight; average weight converts counts (Fourth).
- **Shelf-to-sheet** (Yellow Dog says sheet-to-shelf) = the count list in the physical order of the shelves (https://docs.restaurant365.com/docs/shelf-to-sheet-inventory).
- **Perpetual vs periodic**: a running balance moved by every purchase, sale, waste and transfer (MarketMan, Apicbase) vs purchases expensed and usage derived from counts (R365).
